The provided `fuel-prices.csv` has a station ID, name, address, city, state and retail price. No coordinates. Addresses look like `I-44, EXIT 283 & US-69`, which no free geocoder resolves reliably. So stations are placed at the centroid of their city, using the US Census Gazetteer, once, in `manage.py load_stations`, and moved to a geocoded address where a one-time Geoapify pass found a trustworthy one (`data/station_coords.csv`, committed, so no key is needed).

## Pipeline

| Step | Rows | What happens |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Read CSV | 8,151 | Raw rows. |
| Dedupe by `OPIS Truckstop ID` | 6,738 | Duplicate IDs collapse; the cheapest price wins. |
| Keep US states | 6,626 | Canadian rows (BC, ON, AB, SK…) are out of scope. |
| Match city to Gazetteer | **6,443** | **97.2%** placed. The rest are logged and dropped. |
| Override from `station_coords.csv` | 741 | **11.5%** moved from the city centroid to a geocoded address. |

## Matching

`load_stations` downloads two 2024 Gazetteer files and builds one `(state, normalized name) → (lat, lon)` index:

- **Places** (cities, towns, CDPs) first, then **county subdivisions**, which cover New England towns that are not places.
- **Normalization** lowercases, strips accents (`Cañon` → `canon`), maps `Saint` to `St`, removes the legal suffix (`city`, `town`, `village`, `CDP`…) and collapses spaces, dots and hyphens, so `Mc Calla`, `De Witt` and `Brookpark` all match.
- **Consolidated cities** get alias keys: `Athens-Clarke County unified government` also answers to `athens`, `Boise City city` to `boise`.
- **Precedence:** exact name beats alias, place beats county subdivision, incorporated place beats CDP.
- **Ambiguity:** when two distinct locations tie on all of that and sit more than ~14 miles apart, the key is dropped rather than guessed.

The CSV city is matched unstripped, so `Junction City` never falls through to a place called `Junction`.

## Address geocoding (one-time, offline)

`manage.py geocode_stations` ran once with a Geoapify key and wrote `api/data/station_coords.csv` (`opis_id, lat, lon, source, confidence`). `load_stations` reads it when present; nobody else needs a key.

- **Budget.** 6,443 stations share 6,075 unique address strings. The free tier is 3,000 credits/day and batch geocoding costs 0.5 credit/address plus 1 per job call and poll, so the run sent the 5,000 highest-priority addresses (interstate addresses first, then states with the most stations) for ~2,600 credits. The other 1,075 keep their centroid; re-running the command sends only those.
- **Filter.** A hit is kept only if it is a building, street or amenity with confidence ≥ 0.5, shares a number with the address (`US-259` → `US Highway 259`, `I-35, EXIT 140` → `Interchange 140`; not `Trail Exit`), and lies within 25 mi of the city centroid (guards against a same-named place elsewhere). Everything else keeps the centroid.
- **Yield.** 741 stations (11.5%) moved; median shift 1.7 mi, p90 5.0 mi. Most addresses look like `I-55, EXIT 180`, which the geocoder only resolves to the city, so they keep the centroid.

## Limitations

<Callout title="City centroid, not the station" type="warn">
Except for the 741 address-geocoded stations, a station's position is its city's centroid, a few miles from the actual exit. The 10-mile corridor absorbs most of that error, and mile markers can be off by a few miles. The fix for `I-xx, EXIT nn` addresses is an exit-number lookup (OSM `motorway_junction` nodes).
</Callout>

- **Sparse regions.** The dataset has only 8 California stations, all near the Mexican border. NYC → LA runs its last ~273 miles with no station in reach, which is fine inside the 500-mile range, but it means a plan in the far West has fewer choices than the map suggests.
- **No updates.** Prices are a snapshot. Re-run `load_stations` with a new CSV to refresh; it replaces the table in one transaction and refuses to wipe it if nothing matches.
